Mechanical Repairs & Maintenance

A practical guide to common vehicle systems, service intervals and workshop best practices.

The fundamentals

Repair the cause, not only the symptom

Professional mechanics inspect the whole system, confirm the fault and explain the repair options. Good maintenance can reduce unexpected breakdowns and support safe vehicle operation.

  • Engine performance and cooling-system checks
  • Brake pads, rotors, hydraulic components and brake fluid
  • Suspension, steering and wheel-bearing inspection
  • Transmission, clutch and driveline assessment
  • Oil, coolant, filters and other scheduled services
Maintenance planning

Build a service history

Always follow the owner's manual and the vehicle manufacturer's service schedule. The table below is a general guide, not a replacement for model-specific instructions.

SystemWhat technicians inspectWhy it matters
BrakesPad/rotor wear, fluid condition, leaksStopping performance and safety
EngineOil level, leaks, belts, coolingReliability and temperature control
TiresTread, pressure, wear patternGrip, handling and fuel efficiency
BatteryState of charge and terminalsReliable starting and electrical supply
Safety note: Repairs involving brakes, airbags, high-voltage systems or vehicle lifting should be handled by properly trained people using approved procedures and equipment.
